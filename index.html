Why this exists

A note from me.

"I made this because I needed it."

I live in Vienna but study in Krems. That means almost two hours on the train every morning and another two every evening to get back. On weekends I work. Between all that, meeting new people in my own city felt like something that happened to everyone else.

So I started collecting the places where people here actually meet: the language nights, run clubs, game evenings and volunteer groups. This guide is that list, tidied up so you don't have to start from zero like I did. If you go somewhere because of it, I'd love to hear how it went.

— The person behind Find Your People

Know your rights

€551.10

Marginal job limit, 2026

Earn up to this per month and you're "geringfügig" employed: no social insurance or tax deducted, but you're still covered by accident insurance. Above it, you're fully insured.

Home / Going alone

A gentle start

Going alone.

Everyone at these events was new once. Six things that make the first time easier.

01 / Pick an activity, not a party

Games, runs, choirs and volunteering give everyone something to do, so you never have to invent small talk from nothing.

02 / Go twice before judging

The first visit is always the awkward one. On the second, people recognise you, and that's when it starts to feel like yours.

03 / Tell the host you're new

Most organisers love newcomers and will introduce you to someone. One sentence at the door does a lot of work.

04 / Arrive early, not late

Joining a group of five is easy. Joining a group of forty already deep in conversation is hard.

05 / Have one easy question

"How long have you been coming here?" works everywhere and gets people telling you the good stuff.

06 / Suggest the next one

Friendships grow from repeated, easy contact. "Are you coming next week?" beats a vague "let's hang out".
